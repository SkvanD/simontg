Black Walnut Split-Lid Coffee Table

Material: Black Walnut — 4/4 rough, surfaced to 15/16"  |  Overall: 31½" × 31½" × 18" H  |  Storage depth: 12"

1. Project Overview & Specifications

Solid black walnut replica of an 18" square farmhouse-style coffee table with butterfly split-lid storage. The original is particle board — this version is all-solid walnut with glued-up panels, an applied X-cross face frame on all four sides, and a center-hinged split lid giving access to 12" of interior storage from either side of the table.

  • Overall dimensions: 31½" × 31½" × 18" H
  • Storage cavity: approx. 30" × 30" × 12" deep
  • Lid: Split centre — two panels (15" each), hinged at a 1½" centre rail. Each half lifts independently. Soft-close lid stays prevent slamming.
  • Face frame: Applied X-cross barn-door panel on all four sides. 2" border (stiles & rails), 1½" diagonal braces, half-lapped at the crossing.
  • Corner joinery: 45° mitered corners on box walls and plinth — all four exterior faces are identical width for symmetrical face frames.
  • Finish: Dark walnut oil or General Finishes Gel Stain (Java) + hard wax topcoat to match original espresso tone.
  • Wood: Black walnut (Juglans nigra) — 4/4 rough, surfaced to 15/16". All panels glued up from narrower boards.

5. Step-by-Step Build Guide

Step 1 — Mill & Acclimate Lumber

  1. Rough-cut all boards 1" overlength and ½" overwidth to allow for jointing/planing.
  2. Joint one face flat, plane to 15/16" on the 13" planer.
  3. Joint one edge straight. Rip opposite edge parallel on the table saw.
  4. Stack stickered in the shop for 48 hours minimum to acclimate before glue-up — walnut moves after milling.

Step 2 — Glue Up Panels

  1. Box walls (Part A): For each of the 4 panels, edge-join two boards to reach at least 14¼" wide. Alternate grain direction (cup toward, cup away) to balance movement. Use cauls to keep glue-up flat.
  2. Box bottom (Part B): Glue up 4 boards to reach at least 30¼" × 30¼". Alternate grain direction.
  3. Lid panels (Part F): Two separate glue-ups. Each panel needs two boards to reach 15¼" wide. Choose your best, most quartersawn boards here — the lid is the most visible surface.
  4. Allow 24 hours full cure before handling.

Step 3 — Cut Panels to Final Size & Miter Box Walls

  1. Scrape and sand glue lines flush (card scraper first, then 120 grit).
  2. Cross-cut all 4 box wall panels (Part A) to exact 31½" length.
  3. Rip each panel to exactly 14" wide on the table saw with a sharp rip blade.
  4. Set the table saw (or mitre saw) to 45°. Miter both ends of each wall panel. Cut a test piece first and verify the 4 miters close tightly before cutting real stock.
  5. Cut plinth rails (Part E) to 31½" with 45° miters on both ends.
  6. Cut lid panels (Part F) to exactly 31½" × 15". Square cut — no miters on lid panels.
  7. Cut box bottom (Part B) to 30" × 30". Square cut.
Miter tip: Cut all miters at slightly over 45° (45.5°) so the outside corner closes tightly even if the box distorts slightly during clamping. The inside corner gap is hidden inside the box.

Step 4 — Assemble the Box

  1. Dry-fit all 4 mitered walls using band clamps. Check that the assembly is square (diagonal measurements must match). Adjust miter angles if needed.
  2. Apply Titebond II to all 8 miter faces. Clamp with band clamps and 4 corner blocks. Check square again immediately after clamping.
  3. While glue is still wet: from inside the box, drill pilot holes and drive 1½" pocket screws through each miter face into the adjacent wall to reinforce. 2 screws per corner, top and bottom.
  4. After 2 hours: glue and screw ledger strips (Part C) to the inside of each wall, 2" above the bottom edge. These support the box bottom.
  5. Drop box bottom (Part B) onto the ledger strips. For solid wood: glue centre of panel only, let edges float to allow seasonal movement. Or leave entirely unglued — the weight of contents keeps it in place.

Step 5 — Assemble the Plinth

  1. Dry-fit the 4 plinth rails (Part E) into a square frame. Check diagonals.
  2. Glue miters and clamp. Reinforce with pocket screws from inside.
  3. Once cured, attach plinth to the underside of the box with glue and 1½" pocket screws driven up through the plinth into the box walls.

Step 6 — Mill Face Frame Stock

  1. From your offcuts and wider boards, rip strips to 2" wide for stiles (G) and rails (H), and 1½" wide for X-braces (J). Thickness remains 15/16".
  2. Crosscut stiles (G) to 14". Crosscut rails (H) to 27½".
  3. X-braces (J): Set the miter saw to 20°. Cut both ends of each brace at 20° (parallel cuts, same direction — the brace is a parallelogram, not a trapezoid). Final length along the long edge: approximately 29½". Test-fit in the inner opening before cutting all 8.
  4. Half-laps at crossing: Mark the midpoint of each X-brace. Using the table saw dado stack (or router), cut a 1½" wide × ½" deep notch at the midpoint of each brace. One notch faces up, one faces down — the two braces interlock flat.

Step 7 — Apply Face Frames

  1. Work one side at a time. Start with the two vertical stiles: apply glue to the back face and clamp flush with the outer corners of the box wall. Pin with 1½" brad nails (pre-drill to avoid splitting).
  2. Fit the top and bottom rails between the stiles. Glue and clamp.
  3. Test-fit the X-brace pair (interlocked) in the inner opening. When satisfied, glue the half-lap joint and glue the ends into the corners of the inner opening. Clamp or pin with brads. Hold flat until cured.
  4. Repeat for all 4 sides.
  5. After curing, flush-trim any proud glue joints with a card scraper.

Step 8 — Fit Centre Hinge Rail & Lid Panels

  1. Cut centre hinge rail (Part D) to exactly fit between the inside faces of two opposite box walls (28½" or measure your actual box interior). The rail must be positioned at exactly the mid-point front-to-back — measure from both front and back walls to confirm 15" from each.
  2. Glue and screw the rail to the interior side walls. Ensure its top face is perfectly flush with the top edge of the box walls — use a straightedge across the top to verify before the glue sets.
  3. Lay one lid panel face-down on a flat surface. Position the piano hinge along its inner edge (the edge that will sit over the centre rail). Mark, pre-drill, and screw one leaf to the panel underside.
  4. Set the panel in position, resting on the box walls and the centre rail. Hold the open hinge leaf against the top face of the centre rail, mark screw positions, pre-drill, and screw down.
  5. Repeat for second lid panel. Open and close both panels to verify smooth operation and that they sit flush when closed.
  6. Fit soft-close lid stays: mount one bracket to each box side wall inner face (near each end), connect arm to underside of corresponding lid panel. Adjust tension.

Step 9 — Sand & Finish

  1. Sand entire piece: 120 → 150 → 180 → 220 grit. Use a random-orbit sander for flat panels; hand-sand with the grain for face frame elements.
  2. Ease all sharp outer edges and corners with 220-grit sandpaper or a block plane — two light passes is enough.
  3. Blow off all dust with compressed air. Wipe with a tack cloth.
  4. Apply finish to all surfaces including inside and bottom to equalise moisture exchange and prevent warping.
