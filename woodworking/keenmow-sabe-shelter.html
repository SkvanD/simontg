Keenmow Sabe — Robot Mower Shelter

Project: Outdoor shelter for Keenmow K1 robot lawnmower and charging base
Material: Pressure-treated pine throughout · 3/4" PT plywood roof
Finish: Oil/shellac primer, 2 coats white exterior latex, white elastomeric coating on roof
Units: Imperial (inches)

1. Project Overview & Specs

Free-standing outdoor shelter for a Keenmow K1 robot mower and its charging base. Located in a south-facing position; opening faces north to minimise direct sun ingress. Site is paving slabs. Three slatted walls provide shade, weather protection and airflow to prevent heat build-up. Roof slopes south so rain drains away from the opening. Painted white throughout to reflect heat and present a finished appearance.

  • Interior dimensions: 32" W × 36" D × 20" H
  • Opening: Faces north · 32" wide × 20" tall
  • Post heights: Front (north) 26½" · Back (south) 20"
  • Roof slope: 6½" drop north-to-south — rain drains away from the opening
  • Rain sensor notch: 3" wide × 8" deep, centred on the front (north) roof edge — exposes the K1's onboard rain sensor when docked
  • Name board: 1×6 fascia across front above opening — paint “Keenmow Sabe”
  • Charging station clearance: Station is 17" W × 32" D × 17" H; shelter gives 7½" side clearance and 4" rear clearance

6. Step-by-Step Build

Step 1 — Cut all parts

  1. Cut 4×4 posts: 2 at 26½" (front), 2 at 20" (back) — all from one 8' board.
  2. Cut 2×4 rails: 2 × 37", 2 × 36", 3 × 32".
  3. Cut 1×4 slats: 3 × 32", 6 × 36".
  4. Cut 1×6 fascia: 1 × 39".
  5. Cut plywood roof panel: 42" × 46" from the 4×8 sheet.

Step 2 — Cut the rain sensor notch

  1. Find the centre of the 42" front edge (21" from each side). Mark it.
  2. Mark 1½" each side of centre — giving a 3"-wide notch.
  3. Mark 8" depth in from the front edge.
  4. Clamp the panel to sawhorses. Using the jigsaw: plunge-cut down each side line, then cross-cut at the 8" mark to release the waste piece.
The notch centres above the K1's rain sensor when the mower is docked. The sensor sits 7" from the opening edge; the 8" notch gives 1" of clearance beyond it.

Step 3 — Lay out post positions on the slabs

  1. Mark a 32" × 36" rectangle on the paving slabs for the post inside corners.
  2. Set post base pads at each corner. Check square by measuring diagonals — they must be equal.
  3. Front two posts face north; back two face south.

Step 4 — Set the posts

  1. Drop front posts (27") in the north base pads and back posts (20") in the south base pads.
  2. Check each post is plumb on two faces with a spirit level before fastening.

Step 5 — Install top rails

  1. Front top rail (32"): screw horizontally between the two front posts flush with their tops at 27".
  2. Back top rail (32"): screw horizontally between the two back posts flush with their tops at 20".
  3. Side top rails (37"): attach one end to the top of the front post (27") and the other to the top of the back post (20"). The rail runs at a slight angle — no bevel cut needed, attach square ends flush to post faces. 2 × 3" screws per end.

Step 6 — Install bottom rails

  1. Fit the back bottom rail (32") between the two back posts, raised ~3" above slab level.
  2. Fit the side bottom rails (36") between the front and back posts on each side, also ~3" above slab level.
  3. 2 × 3" screws per end.
No bottom rail on the north (opening) face — the K1 drives straight through.

Step 7 — Fit the wall slats

  1. Space the 3 slats evenly between the bottom and top rails on each walled face (back, east, west). Pre-drill to avoid splitting.
  2. Back wall slats (32"): ends screw into back posts. 2 × 2" screws per end.
  3. Side wall slats (36"): ends screw into the front and back posts on each side. 2 × 2" screws per end.

Step 8 — Fit the fascia board

  1. Mount the 39" 1×6 fascia board on the outside face of the two front posts.
  2. Bottom of fascia flush with the 20" mark on the posts; top sits at ~25½".
  3. 2 × 3" screws into each post, countersunk.

Step 9 — Fit the roof

  1. Lift the 42" × 46" plywood panel onto the four top rails with the notch at the north (front) edge.
  2. Check overhang: approximately 1½" on each side and 1½" front and back.
  3. Confirm the notch is centred over the front opening.
  4. Screw down into the front and back top rails using 1-5/8" screws at ~8" intervals.

Step 10 — Prime and paint (after drying period)

Wait 2–4 weeks after building before painting to let the PT pine fully dry out.
  1. Apply one coat of oil-based or shellac primer to all surfaces. Allow to dry fully.
  2. Apply two coats of white exterior latex paint to all surfaces, allowing each coat to dry.
  3. Apply white elastomeric roof coating to the top face of the plywood panel only.

Step 11 — Paint the name

  1. Once the final paint coat is fully cured, paint or stencil KEENMOW SABE on the fascia board using exterior-rated outdoor paint.
  2. Dark lettering on the white background reads well from a distance and finishes the shelter off like a proper kennel.
